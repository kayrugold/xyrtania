# Xyrtania: Fire & Ice

## Living Game Design Document

**Document status:** Early design  
**Project status:** Working multiplayer engine and evolving game  
**Current design build:** Draft 0.1  
**Last updated:** July 24, 2026

This is a living document. It describes both established creative direction and
ideas that still require design, prototyping, and playtesting.

### Decision labels

- **Established:** A defining part of Xyrtania's direction.
- **Tentative:** A strong idea that may change during development.
- **Open:** A question that has not yet been resolved.

---

## 1. High Concept

**Established**

Xyrtania is a 3D action MMORPG in which a shipwrecked adventurer explores a
mysterious island, survives its wilderness, forms relationships, builds a home,
and may help create and govern an entire civilization.

The game combines the immediate, character-level experience of an open-world
action role-playing game with survival, construction, social simulation, city
building, technological progression, political leadership, and a strategic
world layer. The player is free to participate in these systems or walk away
from them. The world and its inhabitants continue developing in response.

Major inspirations include:

- *The Elder Scrolls IV: Oblivion* and *Gothic 3* for immersive open-world
  exploration and character-scale adventure.
- *Wurm Online* for survival, construction, and the ability to shape the world.
- *SimCity* and *Age of Empires* for settlement growth and task assignment.
- *Civilization III* for technological development and the strategic map.
- *Europa 1400* for social standing, civic life, influence, and political
  advancement.

These inspirations communicate design intent; Xyrtania will develop its own
identity, systems, terminology, and presentation.

## 2. Player Fantasy

**Established**

The player is an adventurer searching for the legendary island of Xyrtania,
rumored to lie off the coast of Baja California. Their expedition ends in
disaster when their ship, aircraft, hot-air balloon, or other vessel crashes on
the island.

Character creation unfolds during the journey rather than in an isolated menu.
The player meets and interacts with fellow travelers before the crash. These
early choices begin establishing:

- Friendships and rivalries.
- Practical skills and traits.
- Other survivors' opinions of the player.
- The social group that may form after reaching shore.
- The player's earliest opportunities for trust and leadership.

The player begins as a survivor, not a predestined ruler. Through choices,
relationships, ability, and reputation, they may eventually become a mayor,
king, council member, explorer, craftsperson, warrior, outcast, or something
else entirely.

## 3. The Opening Experience

**Established direction; details remain tentative**

The opening journey acts as playable character creation and the first social
test.

An apparently minor choice may create a meaningful consequence. If the player
helps an injured traveler with a bandage before the crash, they might acquire
an amateur first-aid trait. After reaching shore, that knowledge could allow
them to brace the same survivor's broken arm. The survivor remembers both
events.

After the crash, the player chooses whether to remain with the survivors:

- Helping the group creates opportunities to gather resources, establish a
  settlement, build relationships, and earn responsibility.
- Performing well during an early bear attack may place the player in a
  leadership role.
- A leader can begin assigning work and directing the construction of a small
  village.
- Fleeing, refusing to help, or leaving to explore remains valid.
- If abandoned, the survivors may build a village, tower, or other refuge
  without the player.
- The player may return later, but survivors will remember the abandonment and
  may distrust or resent them.

This opening establishes a central rule:

> The world remembers what the player did—and what they refused to do.

## 4. Player Freedom and Primary Activities

**Established**

Xyrtania supports multiple overlapping ways of playing:

- Explore an open 3D world.
- Follow quests, mysteries, and the island's larger story.
- Fight wildlife, monsters, hostile people, and fantastical enemies.
- Survive hunger, thirst, weather, and wilderness hazards.
- Gather materials and craft useful objects.
- Build structures and alter the local environment.
- Establish a home in an NPC-controlled or player-controlled settlement.
- Build relationships with NPCs and other players.
- Trade, specialize, and participate in civic life.
- Found or help develop villages, cities, and civilizations.
- Assign work and guide settlement development when granted authority.
- Progress through technological discoveries.
- Participate in regional politics and warfare.
- Use a strategy map to understand and influence civilization-scale events.
- Ignore leadership and civilization systems in favor of a personal adventure.

The civilization layer must remain optional. A player who wants an
Oblivion-style life of exploration should not be forced to manage a kingdom.
Likewise, a player who earns leadership should be able to affect the island on
a strategic scale without losing the ability to walk through it as an
individual.

## 5. A Living, Autonomous World

**Established**

Xyrtania does not wait passively for the player.

Survivors, settlements, factions, and civilizations can pursue goals without
direct player control. Other groups may found villages while the player's group
is still struggling to survive. Settlements can develop, relationships can
change, and political situations can evolve while the player explores
elsewhere.

Player choices influence this simulation without making the player the sole
cause of every event.

This supports three important outcomes:

1. Decisions have visible consequences.
2. Leaving a situation is itself a consequential decision.
3. Replaying the game can produce a substantially different personal and
   political history.

## 6. Intended Emotional Experience

**Established**

Xyrtania should create a blend of:

- **Danger:** The wilderness, weather, hunger, conflict, and unknown forces are
  genuine threats.
- **Freedom:** The player can go anywhere, join others, leave them, lead, build,
  explore, or follow a private path.
- **Accomplishment:** A dock, lighthouse, home, village, alliance, or kingdom
  should visibly change life on the island.
- **Power:** Leadership is earned through the world and can grow from local
  responsibility into kingship.
- **Survival:** Food, water, weather, injuries, and shelter connect the player
  physically to the environment.
- **Discovery:** Activities lead into new places, systems, relationships, and
  layers of story.
- **Mystery:** The island has magical forces and an unexplained history.
- **Wonder:** Natural beauty, realistic constellations, dramatic skies, and
  fantasy events make the world feel larger than its mechanics.
- **Belonging:** The player can earn a place, home, role, and reputation within
  a living community.

## 7. The Mystery of the Crashes

**Established narrative seed**

On the beach, the survivors encounter a woman who has been studying the area.
She explains:

> "The ships have been crashing here... I don't know why the ships always crash
> here, but they do."

She remains near the site as the settlement develops. Once docks are built and
lighthouses are discovered, ships stop crashing there.

This storyline demonstrates how technological and civic progress can change
the world's behavior, not merely unlock bonuses. It also raises an unresolved
question: are the crashes natural, magical, deliberate, or the result of
something the island's inhabitants do not yet understand?

## 8. Fire and Ice

**Established thematic direction; lore details remain tentative**

Fire and Ice are not descriptions of Xyrtania's geography. They represent two
great evils whose conflict threatens the island and may ultimately force
players and NPC civilizations to unite.

One power is presently imagined as a male figure who wields fire; the other as
a female figure who wields ice. They may be tyrants, supernatural rulers,
avatars, or fantasy gods. Their war creates the backdrop for conflicts between
the island's peoples and their leaders.

The conflict should carry moral weight without directly recreating a specific
real-world war. It may echo recognizable tensions:

- Competing powers portraying themselves as righteous.
- Ordinary people suffering for leaders' ambitions.
- Pressure to choose a side when neither side is good.
- Fear, propaganda, loyalty, survival, and resistance.
- Rival peoples discovering that cooperation may be necessary.

Large war scenes may present these beings in the clouds above the battlefield,
accompanied by powerful Nordic-inspired music. Their appearance should make war
feel mythic and terrifying rather than merely decorative.

## 9. Narrative Memory and the Player's Journal

**Established concept**

The player's journey is recorded in an evolving journal. It should capture
meaningful choices, relationships, discoveries, achievements, failures, civic
roles, and turning points.

Other players may be able to read this history. The journal therefore serves
several purposes:

- A personal record of an open-ended adventure.
- A source of character identity and reputation.
- A way to communicate stories between players.
- Evidence that the world noticed the player's actions.
- A foundation for highly replayable, divergent histories.

Privacy, moderation, permanence, and exactly which entries other players can
read remain open design questions.

## 10. Current Guiding Principles

1. **The world remembers action and inaction.**
2. **Leadership is earned, not selected from a menu.**
3. **The world continues without the player.**
4. **Character-scale adventure and civilization-scale strategy coexist.**
5. **Civilization management is powerful but optional.**
6. **Progress changes the lived world, not only statistics.**
7. **The player creates a history rather than following one fixed identity.**
8. **Replayability comes from consequences, simulation, relationships, and
   freedom—not only randomized content.**
